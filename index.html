One UI 3.0 · System UI

Volume Control Redesign

Rethinking one of the most frequently used system controls on every Galaxy device.

Challenge

The One UI 2.x volume panel expanded into a large control block that covered content and forced users to shift attention away from what they were doing. A control invoked dozens of times a day needed to feel instant, glanceable, and physically connected to the hardware keys.

Approach

  • Redesigned the panel as a slim, vertical slider aligned to the volume keys — matching the user's hand position
  • Progressive disclosure: single slider by default, expanding to full per-channel controls (media, ring, notification, system) only on demand
  • Reduced occlusion of underlying content while keeping touch targets accessible
Outcome: Shipped in One UI 3.0 across the Galaxy lineup. The vertical, key-aligned volume interaction became the standard pattern in subsequent One UI generations.
One UI 3.1 · App Common UI

Share Menu Redesign

A single, consistent sharing surface across Samsung's entire app ecosystem.

Challenge

Sharing is one of the highest-traffic flows on the platform, but the share sheet had grown inconsistent across apps — different layouts, different target ordering, and a confusing mix of direct-share targets, apps, and device-to-device sharing.

Approach

  • Defined a common share sheet spec adopted across Samsung first-party apps
  • Restructured hierarchy: people first, then nearby devices (Quick Share), then apps
  • Designed the Quick Share / Nearby Share surface for device-to-device transfer as a first-class citizen of the sheet
Outcome: Shipped in One UI 3.1 and highlighted in Samsung's official press release. The unified sheet became the baseline sharing pattern for first-party apps.
One UI 8.5 · System UI

Game Booster Redesign

In-game system controls that stay out of the way until players need them.

Challenge

Gaming is where system UI is most intrusive: players need performance control, notifications management, and capture tools — without ever leaving the game or breaking immersion. The existing panel required exiting gameplay to change settings.

Approach

  • Full visual overhaul aligned with One UI 8.5's rounded design language
  • In-context panel: adjust settings, view live stats (FPS / CPU / GPU / RAM), and capture — without exiting gameplay
  • Three color-coded performance modes (Performance / Balanced / Battery Saver) with per-game saved configurations
  • Anti-interference gesture system preventing accidental status-bar pulls, navigation gestures, and assistant triggers mid-game
  • Four notification styles (System / Pop-up / Bubble / Dash pill) tunable for transparency, animation, and sound
Outcome: Shipped in One UI 8.5. Covered by Samsung community press as a complete rework of the gaming experience layer.
